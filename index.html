What Bakhiya Means

Bakhiya — "shadow work" — is chikankari's signature stitch, and its most difficult. It's worked entirely from the wrong side of the fabric, in a herringbone path the embroiderer can't see from the front. Held up to the light, the thread falls into a soft, translucent shadow on the right side — a technique unique to chikankari, found nowhere else in Indian embroidery.

Every Shahnisa piece is hand-embroidered by artisans in Lucknow, most of them women working from home — the same cottage-industry model that has sustained this craft for four centuries.

One Tradition

Persia

Shadow-work embroidery

Chikankari (Persian for "embroidery" or "delicate needlework") traces its roots to Persia.

Mughal
Darbar

Noor Jahan

Brought to India by the Mughal empress Noor Jahan, wife of the emperor Jahangir, who was captivated by Persian shadow-work embroidery.

Late 18th
century

Awadh Darbar

Chikankari took root in the darbar (court) of Awadh. From Mughal darbar to Nawabi patronage, it gained fame at royal courts as art delicately embroidered by hand on fine cloth.

Today

Lucknow

Thousands of artisans across Lucknow's villages dance their fingers on fabric to create woven art. Shahnisa brings hand-woven art, chikankari, to you.
