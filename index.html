What Bakhiya Means

Bakhiya, shadow work, is chikankari's signature stitch. The most difficult stitch to stitch, bakhiya is worked entirely from the reverse side of the fabric in a herringbone path and the embroiderer cannot see from the front. Held up to light, bakhiya thread falls into a soft, translucent shadow on the right side. Bakhiya is unique to chikankari.

Every Shahnisa piece is hand-embroidered by artisans in Lucknow. Women chikankari artisans work from home to carry this ancient cottage-industry through 4 centuries.

Persia

Shadow-work embroidery

Chikankari (Persian for "embroidery" or "delicate needlework") traces its roots to Persia.

Mughal
Darbar

Noor Jahan

Brought to India by the Mughal empress Noor Jahan, wife of the emperor Jahangir, who was captivated by Persian shadow-work embroidery.

Late 18th
century

Awadh Darbar

Chikankari took root in the darbar (court) of Awadh. From Mughal darbar to Nawabi patronage, it gained fame at royal courts as art delicately embroidered by hand on fine cloth.

Today

Lucknow

Thousands of artisans across Lucknow's villages dance their fingers on fabric to create woven art. Shahnisa brings hand-woven art, chikankari, to you.
